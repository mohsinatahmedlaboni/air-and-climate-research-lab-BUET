Who we are

Air quality, atmospheric science and climate research at BUET

The Air and Climate Research Lab (ACRL) at Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology works on atmospheric science, air quality and climate.

It combines field observations, environmental monitoring, laboratory analysis and computational modeling to improve understanding of air pollution and regional atmospheric conditions. [Add: when the lab was founded, and its mission in one line]

How we work

Four connected steps, from collecting data to explaining it.

  1. 01

    Field observations

    Measurements across urban, rural and near-source sites in Bangladesh.

  2. 02

    Environmental monitoring

    Reference-grade, chemically speciated and low-cost sensor networks.

  3. 03

    Laboratory analysis

    Chemical analysis of PM₂.₅ to identify pollution sources.

  4. 04

    Computational modeling

    Emission inventories, chemical transport and data-driven models.

Research / Project

Developing Tools to Improve Air Quality Management in Bangladesh

To give Bangladesh the data, models, and trained people needed to design and track air quality mitigation.

Overview

The project builds the tools and local expertise Bangladesh needs to plan and track cost-effective air quality management.

It develops a national emission inventory, a source-resolved intervention model, chemically speciated PM₂.₅ monitoring, nationwide low-cost sensor measurements, and training and public data for government and professionals.

What the project does

01

National emission inventory

High-resolution estimates of emissions from major and minor sources, mapped across Bangladesh.

02

National source-resolved air pollution intervention model

A model that links sources to pollution levels, to test interventions before they are put in place.

03

Chemically speciated PM₂.₅ monitoring and source apportionment

Using the chemistry of PM₂.₅ from 10 speciation sites to find out where pollution comes from.

04

Nationwide low-cost sensor measurements

About 50 low-cost sensor sites feeding data-driven air quality models.

05

Knowledge to action

Training for Department of Environment staff and local professionals, public data and community outreach.

Chemical speciation sites and low-cost sensors placed across urban, rural and near-source locations in Bangladesh.

Graduate Student Fellowship for Air Pollution Research in Bangladesh

Research / Project

Development of Mobile Air Quality Monitoring Unit for High-Resolution Air Pollution Mapping

A vehicle-mounted monitoring unit that maps air pollution at finer spatial resolution than fixed stations can provide.

Overview

The project builds a mobile air quality monitoring unit and uses its measurements to map air pollution street by street.

It covers development and calibration of the mobile system, high-resolution pollution mapping and model validation, machine learning and GIS-based air quality modeling, and field monitoring for empirical model development.

What the project does

01

Development and calibration of mobile monitoring systems

Building the vehicle-mounted unit and calibrating its instruments so its readings can be trusted.

02

High-resolution pollution mapping and model validation

Turning on-road measurements into fine-grained pollution maps and using them to check model results.

03

Machine learning and GIS-based air quality modeling

Combining mobile measurements with spatial data to estimate air quality between measured streets.

04

Field monitoring and empirical model development

Field campaigns that collect the measurements behind the project's empirical models.

Monitoring routes

Routes driven by the mobile monitoring unit to build high-resolution pollution maps.
