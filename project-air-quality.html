Research / Project

Developing Tools to Improve Air Quality Management in Bangladesh

To give Bangladesh the data, models, and trained people needed to design and track air quality mitigation.

Overview

The project builds the tools and local expertise Bangladesh needs to plan and track cost-effective air quality management.

It develops a national emission inventory, a source-resolved intervention model, chemically speciated PM₂.₅ monitoring, nationwide low-cost sensor measurements, and training and public data for government and professionals.

What the project does

01

National emission inventory

High-resolution estimates of emissions from major and minor sources, mapped across Bangladesh.

02

National source-resolved air pollution intervention model

A model that links sources to pollution levels, to test interventions before they are put in place.

03

Chemically speciated PM₂.₅ monitoring and source apportionment

Using the chemistry of PM₂.₅ from 10 speciation sites to find out where pollution comes from.

04

Nationwide low-cost sensor measurements

About 50 low-cost sensor sites feeding data-driven air quality models.

05

Knowledge to action

Training for Department of Environment staff and local professionals, public data and community outreach.

Monitoring network

Chemical speciation sites and low-cost sensors placed across urban, rural and near-source locations in Bangladesh.

Part of this project

Graduate Student Fellowship for Air Pollution Research in Bangladesh

Supports graduate students across Bangladesh to carry out thesis or dissertation research on air pollution. Over the three-year project, about 30 students are supported, up to 10 per annual cycle, selected competitively.

Eligible: current MS or higher-level graduate students at any recognized public or private university in Bangladesh, with a supervisor at their home university.
